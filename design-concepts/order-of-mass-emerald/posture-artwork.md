# Mass posture artwork

Six transparent illustrations were generated with the built-in image-generation tool and optimized to 192 × 192 PNGs for the reader’s 30 dp posture badges. The UI tints them using the active theme’s gold so they remain consistent in both appearances.

## App assets

Folder: `shared/src/commonMain/composeResources/drawable/`

## Prompt set

Shared direction: one isolated, faceless human posture symbol; thick, smooth champagne-gold (#D6BE7D) outlines with transparent negative spaces; a circular head and gently rounded forms; flat clip art readable at 32 px; centered full figure with padding; genuinely transparent background. No realism, skin tones, texture, gradients, shadows, scenery, text, frames, or decoration.

Individual poses:

1. Standing upright, full body, two straight legs and feet together, hands joined at the chest.
2. Seated upright in side profile on a simple chair, hips and knees bent at right angles, feet on the floor, hands resting on thighs.
3. Kneeling in side profile, both knees on the floor, torso upright, hands joined at the chest.
4. Standing in side profile, bowing from the waist with lowered head and joined hands, both feet on the floor.
5. Genuflecting in side profile, right knee on the floor, left foot planted in front, torso upright, hands joined at the chest.
6. Standing facing the viewer, right fingertips touching the forehead to begin the sign of the cross, left hand resting on the chest.

## Placement

Cues describe the congregation’s posture, independently of the reader or priest named in a section. Standing is used for the introductory rites and Gospel; sitting for the readings before the Gospel, the homily, and preparation of the gifts. The Eucharistic Prayer has a kneeling cue with a consecration/local-custom note appropriate to the selected calendar profile. The Creed has a bow cue at the Incarnation, replaced with genuflection on Christmas and the Annunciation. The sign of the cross appears at the beginning and blessing. Communion and the optional Sequence have no fixed posture cue. Sheet view shows posture changes rather than repeating an unchanged cue at every heading.

References: [GIRM chapter II, §43](https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/general-instruction-of-the-roman-missal/girm-chapter-2), [GIRM chapter IV, §137](https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/general-instruction-of-the-roman-missal/girm-chapter-4).
